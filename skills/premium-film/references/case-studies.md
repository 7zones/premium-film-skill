# Case studies: four films, what they became, and why

Use these to choose a structure, a length and a look, and to calibrate what "done" looks like. They are real productions (names withheld), each refined over many rounds with a demanding owner and client. Each lists the brief, the final shape, what was rejected along the way, and the numbers that worked. Two of them shipped in two languages (English and Vietnamese), which is where the per-language notes come from.

## Contents
1. Product showcase A — a brand-protection security product (no narration)

## 1. Product showcase A — a brand-protection security product

**Brief:** "make a 30s motion graphics video to showcase our features… the sky is the limit… keeping our branding and color scheme. Dark mode, primary green. Cybersecurity, premium feel." Then: "now gen a better video with more animations… better soundtrack. Don't make it look repetitive… don't try forcing anything to fit into 1 minute, it can be a bit over… focus on the 4 main modules, but don't limit to only those."

**Final:** 94.6 s, English and Vietnamese cuts + captioned cuts, 1080p60, text-only brand (the short product name in the hook, the full name at the end — the owner removed the logo and the mascot), fictional customer "YourBrand".

| Time | Scene |
|---|---|
| 0–8.8 | Dotted globe with incident pins: "Threats never sleep." → a hex shield forms: "Neither does <product>." → dive |
| 8.8–19.8 | Data Exposure: a 3D tunnel of leaked records, a scanner, a code window with the exposed key → "email alert sent" |
| 19.8–33 | Impersonation: real site vs clone, a wall of 43 lookalikes taken down; Executive Protection score ring |
| 33–44 | Reputation: a 3D wall of posts, a scam narrative detected, a surge line climbing out of frame |
| 44–52.8 | "WHO'S BEHIND IT?" slam: actor graph, IoC verdict, ransomware feed → collapse into a point of light |
| 52.8–66 | Email alerts on a lit stage: the real alert template turned in 3D with a floor reflection and leader lines, a phone lock screen, delivery tracking, a report flying along light arcs to To/CC/BCC |
| 66–79.2 | Real-time API: a terminal types a REST call, JSON returns, then a three.js network with bloom carries signed webhooks to SIEM/SOAR/ticketing/chat; camera flies into the core |
| 79.2–88 | AI Agent: the core becomes the agent; typed question (30 cps), modules feed in, answer card |
| 88–94.6 | Module names converge into the shield; wordmark decodes letter by letter; tagline |

**Rejected or corrected:** pill labels and chapter chips ("look cheap"); a graph that slid instead of drawing; fast section → slow section; a busy 3D intro replacing the globe the owner liked ("the old intro looks better… too fast I can't read"); typing at 52 cps; flat email/API scenes ("too simple and bland" → lit stage + 3D hero objects); 25% slower pace ("too slow" → ~10%); a wordmark decode on only the last letters; a letter that never stopped scrambling.

**Numbers:** PACE = 1.1 s per timeline unit (≈109 bpm), the whole film and score scale with it; scenes 6–12 s; hero results held ≥ 3 s; captions ≤ ~19 characters/s; score: FluidSynth orchestra + synth, D minor → D major, −14 LUFS.

## 2. Product showcase B — a threat-intelligence platform

**Brief:** "a quite different direction… same level of premium, professionally designed, branding-oriented… dark mode, both languages, showcase the modules, use <company> branding" (and permission to capture dashboard screens).

**Final:** 79.5 s, English and Vietnamese, 1080p60 + 30 fps phone copies under 30 MB.

| Time | Scene |
|---|---|
| 0–6 | Light streams converge: "Connect every signal. Investigate every threat." → dive into the light |
| 6–9.5 | A glass hexagon condenses into the mark; the name and "TI PLATFORM" (sized to span mark + name) |
| 9.5–13.5 | The mark divides into a honeycomb of 18 module cells in their sidebar colours; the camera flies through one cell |
| 13.5–49.5 | Six modules (6 s each), trucking along one continuous stage: Data Leaks (constellation → a leaked credential lifts out), Threat Feeds (8,642 signals through a glass prism → 12 incidents), Ransomware (a leak post with a live countdown, a supplier match), VIP Protection (three safety-score rings, an exposure, a takedown), IoC Analyzer (14 sources around a radar sweep → Malicious, 92), Entity 360 (a 3D actor network, focus on one actor) |
| 49.5–61.5 | Investigations: a lens opener, a case board with a progress rail and an evidence chain |
| 61.5–71.5 | Deep AI Agent: typed question, eight modules light up, a structured answer held long enough to read |
| 71.5–79.5 | Every cell gathers and collapses into the mark; full name, tagline, the product's URL |

**Rejected:** the first cut built from real dashboard screenshots with a camera moving over them ("forcing too much UI into the video, not letting space for custom designed components… looks too cheap" next to the first showcase). The rebuild made every module a custom scene (four builder subagents from one written brief). Later: intro too long, product line too small, outro must show the full product name and the product URL (not the corporate domain).

**Numbers:** modules 6 s, hero scenes 10–12 s, hand-over overlap 0.6 s, an answer readable ≥ 3 s, text ≥ 17 px, two-tone headlines copied from the landing page.

## 3. Brand film — an anti-scam non-profit

**Brief:** "do your comprehensive research about <organisation>… create a video around 2–3 minutes… technology, people, community, unity, partners, achievements, history, awards, recognition, contributions… premium, polished design motion graphic video with rich sound and visual effects… aim for general audience. I want the best possible results." Client rules that emerged: no AI-generated video or images; no slogan that belongs to a TV programme; natural, native-sounding narration; brisk.

**Final:** 3:41 Vietnamese master, 3:36 English (its own shorter team section), captioned cuts, a second complete version in another voice, all zipped. Narrator: one Gemini TTS voice, a different model per language (the one that read each language best in auditions). Original score composed in code (A minor → C major at the logo, a recurring 5-note motif, a synthesised traditional local instrument for identity).

| Time | Chapter |
|---|---|
| 0–17.5 | The trap: a message on a phone, a link on a laptop, a call; scam pings rise over a real night photo of the city into a storm in its clouds; a quote; the city's lights go out |
| 17.5–61.7 | The founder: one point of light; his real portrait while he is named; the light draws the logo; the camera draws back and the founding team opens portrait by portrait as each is named (anonymous co-founders as star-filled silhouettes); a closing line about the shared promise with a payoff on each key word; dive through the logo's star |
| 61.7–112.55 | Technology: the protection engine; the browser extension (fake bank page, THEN the block, held to read); the AI check (verdict and reasons one at a time); connections with global organisations; the same scam site flagged, blocked and taken down "within 1–24 hours"; two big numbers resolved by focus |
| 112.55–127 | Real-or-fake game: the opening's three traps return as rounds; green for real, red for fake |
| 127–143.1 | People: a constellation of the team's real faces; each role as it is spoken; "volunteering their expertise" |
| 143.1–163.4 | Community: real photos of the booth and talks; reports drop into a pipeline (report → experts → warning); thousands of lights rise into a dome: "Safety for everyone." — the music lands home as the dome closes and carries on |

**Rejected or corrected (in order):** AI stills and AI video of the city ("too fake", three rounds; the whole footage pipeline was thrown away); crops that cut the founder's head; text-only milestones ("minimise displaying only texts"); a weird-sounding sentence; a slogan that belonged to a TV programme; a long founder section; a halo around the founder; a jump from "free for everyone" straight into the extension; blue/red game colours; demos too fast; a technical "data is shared" claim; an unnatural list sentence; a build that "drops weirdly" into the next section; a scene that "feels distant"; a title flickering by a pixel; a badge that couldn't be proven; the scam caller shown as a real agency (now named with "signs of impersonation"); a co-founder section "introduced instantly… not smooth… too long"; a time-stretched read ("forcing speed"); a 99-second high-pitched hiss; a new line "kinda young, not the correct voice"; English names "pronounced so badly"; the English team section "awkwardly long"; captions in two lines; a "natural" second voice "without emotion".

**Numbers:** 26–28 narration lines per language; breaths 1.2–1.7 s; demos held ~3 s; numbers resolved by focus; captions one line ≤ 42 characters; mix −14 LUFS / ≤ −1 dBTP, music ~8 dB under the voice; release ~75 min for four 60 fps masters.

## 4. Event opener — a national media and data-security workshop

**Brief:** a client's 3-minute script for a national media and data-security workshop's opening (four parts, each with visual, SFX, music and narration directions); "use Gemini voice… Codex for image gen… strive for the best possible quality… Gemini 3.8 Flash to verify… I don't want 'good enough', I want perfect, top notch, pixel perfect." Then: "the video will consist of lots of logos… it's gonna be a disaster if you got just 1 logo wrong."

**Final:** 2:20 in Vietnamese (139.65 s), a subtitled version with no duplicated words, an .srt, a poster; Version 1 kept. Narrator: a deep Gemini TTS voice with "power from conviction, rhythm and emphasis". Music: two licensed Pixabay tracks cut on their bar grids with three stop-time hits, a spectral-freeze bridge and a 6-bar build into the emblem.

| Time | Section |
|---|---|
| 0–28.2 | The Earth at night (NASA city lights, data arcs, broadcast rings) → a dive into a fibre tunnel → "5,700,000 GB every second" → the data gathers into a crystal heart over a mirror floor, glass screens rise around it playing each platform; the camera plunges into the heart |
| 28.2–63.5 | Out of a white flash, a tape archive rebuilt in depth from a photo: a lamp searches the shelves, a wash of light races down the aisle, the shelves burn into sparks that print a film strip frame by frame; a typed query finds the shot in 1.8 s; the shot folds into a beam through a glass prism onto five platforms; the music stops on a turning steel blade (one edge cool, one red); fake news copying itself, a deepfake face of points, a cracking vault, a corridor of pirated copies, a network turning red and collapsing to an ember |
| 63.5–105.9 | The ember stretches into a gold line that opens on real broadcast frames rebuilt in depth; "classified asset / legal evidence / a weapon"; a storm of frames snaps into a rotunda library; a fake post is scanned, split into layers, the original pulled out in gold, the fake shattered; a gold core sealed by two geodesic shields that turn away red attacks |
| 105.9–139.7 | The core's light becomes a dawn; three slogans on three stop-time hits (the camera flies through each); the third chord frozen and handed to a heroic build; four glass monoliths rise from the floor on the beat with the partners' official logos; they come apart into gold that lands in the event's official emblem on the climax; the welcome and the official workshop title; the music runs into its final hit on the bar after the last word |

**Rejected or corrected:** the first, flat 2.5D cut ("kinda basic… merely following the script"); blocky photo dissolves ("this part glitches, transition so weird"); a photo that "just shows there for 3 s"; a film strip that "appears instantly"; depth photos with "shadows"; a photo over its card's border; cheap section titles; four cards static for 10 s; a missed brief line (epic climax with stop-time breaks); an over-exaggerated, too-long climax with "mute then boom"; the client's "the narration has no impact yet… 1–2 s of dead picture"; a new, brighter voice the owner disliked ("keep the old voice… more punchy, more emotion"); a hard switch between music tracks; a dawn that lingered before the slogans; "AI" read wrongly in Vietnamese until it was respelled in the transcript; headlines duplicated by the subtitles.

**Numbers:** 16 narration lines (95–106 s of speech) → 2:20; no still stretch over 1 s except the final logo/title (~4 s); build ~6 bars (~11 s at 125 bpm); slogans ~1.7 s apart; ring-out 4–5 s; voice ~9 dB over music; narrator median F0 ~105–125 Hz; −14 LUFS, −1.2 dBTP; LED-wall type 90–130 px.

## 5. Patterns across all four

- Every first cut was too literal, too flat, or too fast; every final cut had one unifying visual idea, depth, a moving camera, light carried across transitions, and readable holds.
- Every film's timing lived in one timeline file that the score, the narration and the captions read.
- Owners judged sound as much as picture: a consistent narrator, a score that lands and carries, no glitches.
- Truth and brand accuracy were non-negotiable: real photos for real organisations, fictional data for product demos, official names and logo files only, every claim fact-checked.
- Owners asked "is it done?" often: always say what is done, what is still rendering, where the files are, and which one to send.
- Lengths: product showcases 80–95 s; a brand film grew from "2–3 minutes" to 3:41 as the client added content (accepted, but every section stayed brisk); an event opener filled what its narration needed (2:20 of a "3-minute" script).

## 6. Short teasers (15–45 s): the same rules, compressed

None of the four films was this short; this shape applies their rules to a teaser and was tried on a 30 s and a 15 s test film.
- One idea in three beats: the **hook** (the problem, on screen within the first second; no long fade from black), the **turn** (the product doing its one thing: the hero moment, with the film's biggest hit), the **name** (the lockup and tagline, the music resolving exactly on the name, a 3–4 s ring-out after the last word).
- One short narrated line per beat (or none), ~1 s breath between them; narration fills at most ~60% of the time.
- Holds still apply: a result stays readable ≥ 3 s in total and ≥ ~1 s after its line ends; the name holds longest.
- A 30 s template: hook 0–9 s (line at ~0.8 s), turn 9–20 s (the hit ~11–12 s, its line just after), name 20–30 s (mark ~21 s, line ~21.5 s, ~4 s hold after the last word). Scale for 15 s or 45 s; never cram a feature list in.
- Richness matters more, not less, at this length: three layers of depth in every frame, the hero card or object filling most of the frame, a lit environment behind it (a generated plate if an image service is available) rather than plain black — see "Full, rich frames" in quality-bar.md.
- Music takes one shape: tension → lift on the turn → resolution on the name. The glitch scan's "dead" check matters more, not less: a teaser has no time for still picture.
